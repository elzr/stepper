## Claude:
Screenmaps now tracks the windows rcmd puts away. Under the map, after "Minimized windows", there's a new "rcmd-hidden windows" strip, and the list below has a matching section. Each window shows its app's rcmd key (⌥T Telegram, ⌥W WhatsApp, ⌥G Figma, and so on). Right now it shows six: Photos, Activity Monitor, Safari, WhatsApp, Telegram and Figma, each on the right display.

**Hover preview, for both sections:** hovering a minimized or hidden window, either its chip under the map or its row in the list, shows a dashed outline on the map where the window will come back, on top of its display's pile. It says "⌥T brings it back here" or "comes back here from the Dock". I checked this in the browser with the map flat and turned, from both the chip and the list row.

**How it works:**
- **Reading them:** the off-main-thread helper (`lua/layoutsnap.swift`) now also reads hidden apps' windows. It gets each app's key straight from rcmd's own preferences, matched by app path.
- **Following your toggles:** hiding or showing an app now saves the layout within 3 seconds, so the page keeps up with your rcmd presses instead of waiting for the once-a-minute save. I tested this by hiding and showing Preview, which had no windows open, so nothing moved on your screens.
- **Bringing one back:** ⌘-clicking a hidden window on the page shows its app again.

**Details worth knowing:**
- **Not only rcmd:** the page can't tell whether rcmd or ⌘H hid an app. An app hidden with ⌘H, or one with no rcmd key, still appears in the section, just without a key.
- **A macOS quirk:** while an app is hidden, macOS labels its windows as dialogs rather than normal windows (Figma was the exception). The save's filter for stray windows would have dropped five of the six. Hidden windows are now judged only by size and title.

The screenmaps README is updated, and the work is committed and pushed as `e4103b6`.
