## What's actually happening

Your Mac **isn't reading the display's EDID at all** — the UUID is all zeros and the product name is blank. EDID is the little data blob every display sends over the HDMI cable saying "I'm a 4K panel, here are my modes." With no EDID, macOS falls back to a generic safe profile and only offers 1080p/720p. That's your Image #1.

The key insight is that **your two symptoms are one root cause.** EDID and DDC/CI (the channel Lunar uses for brightness) **both ride the same low-speed I²C side-channel** in the HDMI connector (pins 15/16 = clock/data, pin 19 = hotplug). The high-bandwidth video lanes are separate wires. So right now:

- **Video lanes:** working → you still get a picture, just generic 1080p
- **I²C/DDC side-channel:** dead → no EDID (no 4K) **and** no DDC (Lunar goes "non-responsive," falls to software dimming)

That's also why Lunar shows the display with an *empty name* (`Non-responsive display ""`) — it reads the same blank ProductName. Same failure, two faces. This is **not** Lunar's fault and not a macOS setting; reinstalling/toggling software won't fix it.

Why it died on sleep/wake with no cable change: the link **re-handshakes from scratch on wake**, and the I²C channel failed to come back. A connection that holds fine once established can fail to re-negotiate.

## Fix, highest-yield first

1. **Power-drain the monitor.** Unplug its power cable *from the wall* (not just standby/off), wait ~60s, plug back in. This resets the display's HDMI controller and forces a fresh EDID handshake — it's the single most effective fix for "EDID went blank after wake," especially on a TV-class panel.
2. **Reseat the HDMI cable**, firmly, both ends — or move it to the monitor's *other* HDMI port. Fresh handshake + different physical pins. Loose/oxidized DDC pins are a classic cause.
3. **Hotplug the Mac side** — unplug/replug the HDMI connector to trigger a re-detect.
4. **If still 1080p, swap the cable** even though you didn't change it. Sleep/wake is *exactly* when a marginal cable tips over — it carried 4K yesterday on an already-trained link but can't re-read EDID on a cold re-handshake. Use a known HDMI 2.0/2.1-certified one. → If you're going through a **USB‑C/Thunderbolt→HDMI dongle** rather than the MacBook's built-in HDMI port, suspect the dongle here too; it's an extra link in the I²C chain and a common failure point.
5. Once EDID is back, glance at the monitor's **OSD** for an HDMI "Enhanced / UHD Color / Deep Color / HDMI 2.0" per-input toggle and make sure it's on (a power blip can reset it).

Your machine is an M1 Max (16" MBP); its built-in HDMI is HDMI 2.0, so 4K@60 is well within spec — the panel and port are fine, it's the handshake that broke.

That's the **generic HDMI baseline set** (480p/576p/720p/1080p) — exactly what macOS hands you when it gets *no* EDID. There's no 4K entry to select, which is why displayplacer (or any "set resolution" tool) can't force it: you can't pick a mode that isn't in the list, and macOS won't synthesize 4K timings for a panel it can't identify. It's even labeling it a *"12 inch"* screen with serial `s0` — it has zero data about the actual display.

## So is it the cable? Most likely — here's the precise reasoning

You've now ruled out the easy stuff (monitor power-drain, reseat both ends, Mac hotplug) and EDID is *still* all-zeros. Reseating fixes a **loose** connector; it does nothing for a **degraded** one. The EDID/DDC lines are low-speed I²C pins that can carry fine on an already-established link and then fail to re-negotiate *from cold* — and sleep/wake is precisely the event that re-runs that cold handshake. That's your "nothing changed but it broke": a latent fault the sleep exposed, not random.

## Two free things genuinely worth trying first

1. **Full cold shutdown** (Apple menu → Shut Down, wait ~30s, power on) with the monitor connected — *not* a restart. Apple Silicon re-inits its display coprocessor (DCP) far more deeply on a cold boot than on a hotplug, and a hotplug is all you've done so far. Best free shot, ~2 min.
2. **Localize the fault so you don't guess-buy:** plug the Samsung into *any* other source — another laptop, a console, an Apple/Fire TV stick, a phone with HDMI-out.
   - Works there → monitor's fine; it's the **cable** (or your Mac's HDMI port) → a cheap cable fixes it.
   - Still broken there → it's the **monitor's HDMI board/EEPROM** → no cable will help.

If you have a **USB‑C→HDMI adapter** lying around, running the same cable through it into a Thunderbolt port bypasses the Mac's built-in HDMI port — isolates whether *that* port is the dead part.

## What to skip
Software EDID-override (BetterDisplay & co.). On Intel Macs you could inject a fake EDID to unlock 4K; on Apple Silicon M-series that path is SIP-blocked and unreliable for a physical display with a dead handshake. Don't sink time there.

**Bottom line:** a known-good **HDMI 2.0/2.1 cable (~$8)** is the most likely fix and the cheapest — but do the "other source" test first so you *know* it's the cable and not the monitor before ordering. Once you cold-boot or swap the cable, say the word and I'll re-read the EDID to confirm the channel's actually back (real product name + 4K modes returning) rather than eyeballing the menu.
