# [L006/screenmaps](https://stepper.internal/features/L006-layout-restore-of-windows-in-screens/subfeatures/screenmaps/) — a digital twin of the desk

> Each display config's last layout: a map of its displays at their real sizes under a camera you turn, every window a layer stacked above its display or sunk into it, the windows by the day they were opened and last used, and a list laid out as the displays stand. ==🟢Open it on the go to see where every window was on the desk==; at the desk it's a live twin that brings a window forward when you ⌘-click it.

## What it does

==🟢[The page](https://stepper.internal/features/L006-layout-restore-of-windows-in-screens/subfeatures/screenmaps/) shows one display config at a time==, switched at the top right: **quad** (the built-in, two 32″ LGs and two 37″ Samsungs), **single** (the built-in and one external), **dual**, **native** (the laptop alone), each listed once it has a layout. The green dot marks the config in use. ==🔵It opens on the config in use, unless that's the laptop alone==: on the go, it opens on the desk layout you last left. A config picked by hand stays picked until the page is reloaded. ==🟢The start page links it==: the 🖥️ after the em dash on [elzr.internal/start](https://elzr.internal/start/)'s topsight line (since 2026-10-10).

- ==🟢Every save that changes something updates it== ([L006](https://stepper.internal/features/L006-layout-restore-of-windows-in-screens/)'s autosave, every minute, and 3 s after stepper moves a window to another display), and ==🔵the open page follows within 3 seconds== while it's in view. The header says "unchanged since 19:38" for the config in use, "last here Oct 7, 20:25" for the others.
- ==🟣It's a backup you can read, not one that restores==: restoring stays with L006's hotkeys and its automatic restores.
- It follows the [F030 tablogs](https://reading.internal/features/F030-tablogs/subfeatures/Chrome-tablogs/)' look: the same panel switch, day rows, chips, popup and violet front window, so the two maps read as one family.

## The views

==🟢The panel holds *Map* or *Days*== (`m`, `d`), as the tablogs' panel holds *Windows* or *Days*; ==🟢the list is always below it==.

==🟢*Map*== draws each display ==🔵at its real size==, from its EDID's millimetres: the 37″ Samsungs stand taller than the 32″ LGs are wide, though all four are 4K. The displays sit as macOS arranges them, pushed against the ones they touch so the bigger panels neither overlap nor leave gaps, and each label gives the size (`←Left 37″ · 5`). ==🔵Three exceptions, where macOS's numbers aren't the desk==: ==🟢the laptop sits centred under the display above it== (macOS has it at Middle Center's left edge only so the pointer crosses there), ==🟢every column of displays stands on the desk==, its lowest display's bottom on the line of the lowest of all, so Left, Bottom Center and Right end level (until 2026-10-11 each side display sat as high as it had been dragged in System Settings, and all three ended at different heights), and ==🟢twins share a size==: the Middle Center LG's EDID reports 598 × 341 mm, a 27″ panel, where its twin above reports the 702 × 400 both are, so displays of one model at one resolution take the larger reading (`twinSizes`; the hover title keeps what the EDID said). Each window is where it was, back to front, clipped to its display the way macOS shows it, with its app's icon and ==🟢its whole title, wrapped to three lines== (Chrome's " - Google Chrome - Eli…" tail trimmed), a faded icon in the middle of the big ones, and a tint per app. ==🟢Face on each window is see-through below its title bar== (since 2026-10-11), so the windows under it show (for an evening it also thickened each border the farther back the window sat in its stack; undone, 2026-10-11). ==🟢A Chrome window shows its tabs== instead (since 2026-10-11): the Chrome logo on top, a rule, and each tab's favicon in tab order, the front tab ringed, from the [live Chrome tablog](https://reading.internal/features/F030-tablogs/subfeatures/Chrome-tablogs/now/); its popup lists their titles. Only on the config in use, the one the tablog reads.

- ==🟢*Flat | Turned | On the ground*== (`1`, `2`, `3`): ==🔵the map is one scene under a camera you move==, its angles from these presets, each window one layer in front of the one behind it on its display, from the front-to-back order the saves record. ==🟢*Flat*== looks at it face on, a flat map: the windows see-through below their title bars, the labels above them. ==🟢*Turned*== stands the displays upright, turned so their right edges are the farther ones and seen a little from above, so a stack of maximized windows that hides itself face on shows every layer. ==🟢*On the ground*== lays them flat as on a floor, the Left display nearest and the Right one farthest. ==🔵From any of them, drag to turn it, right-drag to pan==, pinch to zoom (trackpad or touch); ⌃-drag and ⇧-drag pan too, and a plain scroll still scrolls the page. ==🔵As you turn it from face on, the windows fill in and the labels sink to their displays== over the first 20°, ==🟢along the line of sight, so they never move on screen== (until 2026-10-12 they rose straight off the plane and drifted sideways and back, up to 34 px, as you turned). On a touch screen, which has no right drag, where you put your finger decides: on a display or a window it turns, on the canvas around them it pans. ==🟢*Turned* is the default on native==, the laptop alone, where most windows are maximized; *Flat* elsewhere. The preset is kept per config; the angles you drag to last the visit.
  - ==🟣How it got here, all on 2026-10-11==: the panel had *Flat | 3D | Free 3D*, three renderers. Free 3D, opened face on at Flat's size and look, turned out to be what the 3D view should have been (the 3D view had its own scale and bigger titles, and drew the plane already turned, so it never read as the flat map it was), and its presets took the toggle's place. Gone with the 3D view: its 51 px corner icons and 22.5 px titles, and its columns moved apart by the stacks' depth.
- ==🟢*Above | Below*== (`s` switches; kept for every config): ==🔵the windows stack up from their display, the front one nearest you (*Above*), or sink into it (*Below*)==. ==🔵In *Above*, turned past edge on so you see the plane from behind, the display surfaces fade to their outlines== (`behindness`), since from there they stand in front of every window; from the front they're behind every window and stay solid. In *Below*, ==🟢each display is a see-through hole as deep as its windows==, its face open, glassy walls folded back from its edges, the floor one layer under the back window, the front window just under the face. Face on it still reads as the flat map: ==🔵the floor stands in for the display's background, and fades to its outline as you turn the scene== (seen from behind too), so it hides nothing past it (until 2026-10-11 it stayed 88% opaque at every angle). Turned or on the ground, the windows sit in their displays like layers in a glass box; ==🟢hovering and ⌘-raising reach them through the open face== (the plane's own surface, at depth 0, had caught the pointer in front of every sunk window).
- ==🟢The canvas is as wide as the browser window==, out past the panel's sides, with a border above and below, and ==🟢as tall as you drag its bottom edge== (a grip shows on hover; kept for every config, until then 68% of the window's height; the scene keeps its size and stays centred).
  - ==🟢`Tab` flies around==: all the displays, then Left, Top Center, Middle Center, Bottom Center, Right (`⇧Tab` back), zooming and panning just enough to fit each, without turning the scene; the controls name where you are.
- ==🔵The front window has tablogs' violet outline==; each display's top window casts a deeper shadow.
- ==🟢*Hidden/Minimized windows* come after a rule== under the displays, centred, as the tablogs' minimized Chrome windows do: ==🟢first the hidden apps' windows==, the ones [rcmd](https://lowtechguys.com/rcmd/)'s second press puts away (Telegram on ⌥T, WhatsApp on ⌥W, Figma on ⌥G), which neither the Dock nor AltTab shows, ==🟢then, after a `|`, the minimized ones== (since 2026-10-12; for an hour that day they were two sections, *Minimized* then *rcmd-hidden*). Each hidden window's chip carries its app's rcmd key, read from rcmd's own preferences by app path; an app hidden with ⌘H, or one rcmd has no key for, shows without one.
  - ==🟢On the map they live under their displays==, dashed, one layer each, its hidden windows nearest: ==🔵in *Above* behind the display's surface, in *Below* under its hole's floor==. Face on the display hides them (the floor, 88% opaque, nearly); ==🟢turn the scene, or look from behind or underneath, and there they are==, each labelled.
  - ==🟢Hovering one, its chip or its row in the list, lifts it to where it comes back==: the top of its display's stack (just under the face in *Below*), saying "⌥T brings it back here" or "comes back here from the Dock" (==🔵that label only while it's lifted==: always on, it was too loud, 2026-10-12), and lets it sink again when the pointer leaves (`--rest`, `--up`). They take no pointer themselves (one rising out from under the pointer would drop straight back), and no part in fitting the scene or in `Tab`'s stops.
  - ==🔵Hiding or showing an app saves within 3 s== (an `hs.application.watcher` in screenmaps.lua calls L006's `triggerSave`), so the page follows rcmd's toggles; ⌘-click on a hidden window shows its app.
- ==🟢Every window a ◆ hotkey toggles says so==, a reminder of the keys you've set (since 2026-10-12): "◆D to toggle" under w42days' title bar, "R⌘◆D to toggle" under w41days' (last week's note), "R⌥◆D" under next week's, "◆Z" under whatever window live slot Z holds, written as [fleet F002's keymap](https://fleet.internal/features/F002-harmonious-keybindings/keymap.html) writes them. The same keys follow the window's title in the list, on its chip, and in its popup. The page reads stepper's own files and matches as [bear-hud.lua](openfile:///Users/sara/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox/projects/log/2025/hammerspoon/stepper/lua/bear-hud.lua) does (`toggleKeys`): a note key's Bear window by its title, with `bear-notes.jsonc`'s week vars filled in; a live slot's by its window id, else its app's window with the recorded title. Re-set a slot with R⌥◆ and the map follows at the next poll.
- ==🟢Hovering a window, on the map or in the list, lights it in both==: its tint deepens with a blue ring on the map and its row turns blue in the list, its display's border and background darken (its section in the list too), and ==🔵a moment later every other window of its app takes a lighter tint==, fading in, so you see where an app's windows are spread. ==🟢The window comes to the top while hovered==: face on at once, as on a flat map; ==🟢turned, only while you hold ⌘==, and without the windows in front of it fading (as they did until 2026-10-11, which kept raising the wrong one): ==🔵it slides straight toward you along the line of sight==. The scene has no perspective, so it stays exactly where it was on screen, under the pointer, and only the depth order changes (`raise`, from the plane's rotation undone). Letting go of ⌘ puts it back.
- ==🟢The app filters, top right of the panel==: each app with several windows (Chrome, kitty, Bear…), its icon and count, most first. A click dims every other window on the map and in the list; a second click or `Esc` shows all again. ==🟢The app's name after each window in the list is the same toggle== (since 2026-10-11), for any app, one window or many: hovering shows it's a button, a click keeps only that app's windows lit, on the map and in the list, a second click shows all. ==🔵The notice naming the pick floats at the bottom of the window==, so it comes and goes without moving the list under the pointer (until 2026-10-11 it was a full-width bar above the list, which pushed the toggle away from a second click).
- ==🟢Hovering a window opens its popup==: the whole title, its app, size and display, when it was opened, moved and last used.
- ==🟢⌘-click a window to bring it forward== (a minimized one back from the Dock), on the map or in the list, on the config in use, on the Mac: through `hammerspoon://screenmaps?focus=<window id>`. ==🔵A plain click did it until 2026-10-11==, then was turned off: it lagged a little, and the map is for seeing what's on the screens; now bringing a window forward is the deliberate choice. ⌘ on a window clicks instead of turning the scene.

==🟢*Days*== puts the windows by day twice, side by side: ==🔵*Opened*==, by the day each was first saved, and ==🔵*Last used*==, by the day it was last brought to the front, moved or minimized. Each app shows once per day with how many of its windows, most first, as the tablogs' sites do; hovering lists them. ==🟢Clicking an app or a day picks out its windows== in the list below and on the map (a banner names the pick; `Esc` clears it).

- ==🔵The windows open before screenmaps began say so== ("Fri Oct 9, already open"): macOS keeps no window's creation time, so *Opened* starts with the first save. From then on every new window gets its day.
- "Last used" is what a save can see: a window that came above the one in front at the last save, moved, appeared, went to the Dock or came back from it. A window used and left between two saves (at most a minute apart while anything changes) can be missed.

==🟢The list== is a bulleted list per display, ==🔵laid out as the displays stand==: the left display's column, the center column (Top, then Middle, then Bottom Center), the right display's column, and *Hidden/Minimized* after a rule: the hidden apps' windows with their rcmd keys, then, after a dotted rule, the minimized ones, each saying the display it comes back to. Each window shows its icon, title and app, front to back; the front window's bullet is violet.

## Where the data comes from

==🔵[lua/screenmaps.lua](openfile:///Users/sara/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox/projects/log/2025/hammerspoon/stepper/lua/screenmaps.lua) writes it==, handed each save that changed something by [layout.lua](openfile:///Users/sara/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox/projects/log/2025/hammerspoon/stepper/lua/layout.lua)'s `fileSnapshot`, from what [layoutsnap.swift](openfile:///Users/sara/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox/projects/log/2025/hammerspoon/stepper/lua/layoutsnap.swift) read off the main thread ([changelog](openfile:///Users/sara/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox/projects/log/2025/hammerspoon/stepper/changelog/2026-10-09-layout-saves-off-the-main-thread.md)): the on-screen windows, every regular app's minimized ones, the hidden apps' windows (each with its rcmd key, from rcmd's `appKeyAssignments`), and each display's size in millimetres (`CGDisplayScreenSize`). ==🟢A save that writes costs about 11 ms of Hammerspoon's main thread==, and one that changes nothing writes nothing here. The running apps are read only at each config change: walking all ~190 processes takes ~40 ms.

| File (in [data/](openfolder:///Users/sara/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox/projects/log/2025/hammerspoon/stepper/data), untracked) | Holds |
|---|---|
| `screenmap-<config>.json` | ==🟢One per config, so each keeps its last layout while you're elsewhere==: every display (position name, monitor, size in mm, full and visible frame, rotation), the windows front to back, the minimized ones and the hidden apps' ones (window id, app, bundle ID, title, display, frame, and when each was first saved, last moved, last used; a hidden one also its rcmd key, `rcmd`), and `since`, when this config's map began |
| `screenmaps-now.json` | the config in use, when each config's screenmap and layout file were last written, and the running apps' bundle IDs by name |
| `chrome-now.jsonc` | ==🔵a symlink to the live Chrome tablog's [now/chrome.jsonc](openfile:///Users/sara/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox/projects/log/2026/reading/features/F030-tablogs/subfeatures/Chrome-tablogs/now/chrome.jsonc)==, so the page reads it from its own site (reading.internal sends no CORS header for it); its windows' `bounds` match the saves' frames, its tabs' `fav` names their favicons in reading.internal's F030-tablogs/favicons/. Polled with the rest |
| `bear-notes.jsonc`, `live-toggle-hotkeys.json` | ==🔵stepper's ◆ keys== (the note keys, with their week vars, and the live slots bear-hud.lua rewrites on every R⌥◆): the page reads them as they are, polled with the rest, for each window's "◆D to toggle" (tracked and kept by stepper, not written for the page) |
| `app-icons/<bundleID>.png` | the icons, shared with [bear-hud.lua](openfile:///Users/sara/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox/projects/log/2025/hammerspoon/stepper/lua/bear-hud.lua) and [keymapwatch.lua](openfile:///Users/sara/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox/projects/log/2025/hammerspoon/stepper/lua/keymapwatch.lua); missing ones are saved as apps show up |

- ==🔵A config not used since screenmaps began== is rebuilt by the page from its `window-layout-<n>.json`, with a note saying so: only the displays that held a window, at their point sizes, no window ids or times, and icons borrowed from the apps the newer maps and the running apps name. Its first use writes a real screenmap.
- ==🔵Times are per window id==, so a retitled window keeps them; a frame within 2 px of the last one hasn't moved. Going to the Dock or being hidden counts as use, and so does coming back.
- ==🔴A hidden app's windows say `AXDialog`, not `AXStandardWindow`== (Telegram, WhatsApp, Safari, Photos, Activity Monitor; Figma's kept its own, 2026-10-12), so the save's ghost filter judges them by size and title alone.
- ==🔴While the screen is locked, macOS gives no windows to anyone== (Hammerspoon's own `hs.window.orderedWindows()` included), and background apps don't answer at all: the helper reports them after its 1 s per call. The save skips as it always has, and the first one after the unlock checks for drift.
- The page fetches its JSON with `?raw=1`, as the tablogs do: Caddy's [F022](https://fleet.internal/features/F022-project-files-open-richly-in-browser/) viewers answer a bare `.json` URL with their viewer page. ==🔵`stepper.internal` serves the whole stepper folder==, so the page needs no Caddy change, and it's reachable from the iPad like every `.internal` site ([F019](https://fleet.internal/features/F019-same-internal-urls-on-all-devices/)).
- ==🟣Checking on it==: `hs -c 'return layout.saveStats()'` for the saves, `hs -c 'return hs.inspect(_G._stepper.screenmaps.lastFocus)'` for the last click.
